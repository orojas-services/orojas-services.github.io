My teaching grew out of communication, language, and the experience of working across both.

My background combines audiovisual communication, translation, adult language instruction, university teaching, and more than five years of work with academic and instructional content in higher education.

Together, those experiences shape how I approach communication: as something that is spoken, written, visual, contextual, and developed through practice.

Communication Foundation

Learning to think across media and forms

My undergraduate studies in audiovisual communication introduced me to communication through writing, theory, research, photography, graphic design, radio, television, scripts, and other forms of media production.

That background taught me to think about communication not only in terms of what is being said, but also how it is presented, who it is intended for, and how medium and context shape meaning.

Those questions remain central to my teaching today, particularly when students work across spoken, written, visual, and audiovisual forms of communication.

Language Foundation

Translation deepened my attention to language and meaning

Graduate study in translation added another dimension to that communication background by requiring sustained attention to language, interpretation, audience, style, syntax, editing, and cultural context.

My coursework included translation into English, advanced Spanish syntax, writing and style, semiotics, legal translation, translation for museums, computer-assisted translation tools, and editing translated texts in English.

Translation reinforced the importance of deliberate language choices: communicating meaning clearly while remaining attentive to purpose, context, and audience.

Development as an Educator

From adult language instruction to higher education

My formal teaching experience began with Berlitz Puerto Rico, where I taught English and Spanish to adult learners in synchronous online settings.

Over nearly 450 hours of instruction, I worked with individuals and groups at different proficiency levels and with different professional communication goals. That experience required continual adjustment of instruction, activities, pacing, and feedback to meet learners where they were.

I later expanded that foundation through TESOL professional preparation and moved into undergraduate teaching at Universidad del Sagrado Corazón, where I now teach English communication in online and face-to-face settings.

Teaching Approach

Communication develops through use

I approach communication as something students develop by doing: speaking, writing, analyzing, discussing, presenting, responding, and revising.

I want students to understand not only what they are communicating, but why particular choices may be more effective for a given audience, purpose, and context. This is especially important in rhetoric and argumentation, where claims, evidence, reasoning, and audience awareness work together.

Assessment is part of that process. Clear criteria, rubrics, formative practice, and targeted feedback can make expectations visible and help students identify what is working and what they need to improve next.

Teaching Beyond the Classroom

Seeing courses from both the instructor and content side

Alongside my teaching work, I have spent more than five years editing, reviewing, and translating academic, curricular, and instructional materials in higher education.

I collaborate with subject-matter experts, curriculum specialists, instructional designers, quality-assurance personnel, and academic teams on university courses and learning resources.

That work has made me attentive to what students actually encounter in a course: the wording of instructions, the sequence of content and activities, the relationship between objectives and assessments, the clarity of learning materials, and the consistency of the experience as a whole.
