About

My language work combines professional editing, translation, research, and higher-education content.

I am an English–Spanish editor and translator with more than five years of experience working with academic and instructional content in higher education. My background combines professional language work with graduate training in translation and experience across course-development workflows.

Professional Background

Editing and translation within content-development teams

My professional work includes editing, reviewing, and translating academic, curricular, and instructional materials across disciplines.

I work with language at both the sentence and document level, paying attention to grammar, terminology, coherence, consistency, tone, accuracy, and the relationship between the content and its intended audience.

I also collaborate with instructional designers, curriculum specialists, subject-matter experts, editors, and quality-assurance personnel as part of broader course-development and review processes.

Education

M.A. in Translation

I earned my Master of Arts in Translation from the University of Puerto Rico, Río Piedras Campus.

My graduate work strengthened my practice in translation, terminology, research, textual analysis, cultural context, and communication across languages and audiences.

My master's thesis, Tuna as Told by and for Tunos, is a commented English translation of Pedro J. Portal Basurco's La Tuna Contada por y para Tunos.

Approach

Language choices should serve meaning and audience.

I approach editing and translation by first understanding what the text needs to communicate, who needs to understand it, and what linguistic, disciplinary, or cultural context matters.

From there, I focus on accuracy, clarity, terminology, coherence, tone, and consistency while preserving the intended meaning and purpose of the original content.

Broader Professional Context

Language work connected to learning and communication

My experience in higher-education teaching and instructional content development also shapes how I approach language work. I regularly consider how information is organized, how readers interpret instructions and explanations, and how language choices affect comprehension.

That perspective is particularly useful when working with academic, instructional, educational, or professional content where the quality of the language directly affects how well the material communicates.
