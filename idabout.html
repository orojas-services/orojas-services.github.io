About

My path into instructional design grew from working directly with courses, content, and learners.

I am a bilingual higher-education professional with more than five years of experience working with academic and instructional content. My path into instructional design combines course-development and quality-assurance work, university teaching, Canvas-based instruction, assessment, and a professional background in English–Spanish editing and translation.

That combination has given me experience with both sides of the learning experience: how courses and instructional materials are developed, reviewed, corrected, and maintained, and how learners actually encounter instructions, activities, assessments, feedback, and course structure in practice.

Course Development

Working inside higher-education course-development and quality-assurance workflows

My instructional design experience developed through sustained work reviewing and refining academic, curricular, and instructional materials in higher education.

I have worked within course-development and review processes involving instructional designers, curriculum specialists, subject-matter experts, editors, quality-assurance personnel, course builders, and academic stakeholders. My responsibilities have included reviewing learner-facing materials for language, terminology, accuracy, coherence, consistency, structure, formatting, instructional quality, and alignment with institutional requirements.

The work is iterative rather than limited to a single editing pass. It can involve identifying a defect, determining what other assets are affected, coordinating or incorporating revisions, checking consistency across course materials, documenting changes, and verifying that the corrected treatment has been carried through the learning experience.

Course Maintenance & Quality Assurance

Reviewing existing course materials for instructional, content, language, formatting, and consistency issues; correcting defects; checking related assets; and helping preserve a reliable learner-facing version of the course.

Canvas & Online Course Review

Working with learner-facing Canvas content and course organization, including modules, assignments, assessments, rubrics, gradebook functions, online discussions, course requirements, and instructional materials used across online and blended environments.

Cross-Functional Review

Collaborating across instructional design, curriculum, subject-matter expertise, editing, quality assurance, and course-building functions to resolve content and instructional issues during development and maintenance.

Bilingual Content Quality

Applying English–Spanish editing and translation experience to terminology, clarity, consistency, audience, meaning, syntax, style, references, and document-level coherence within academic and instructional content.

Teaching Perspective

I also see instructional decisions from the learner-facing side.

As an Adjunct Professor of English Communication at Universidad del Sagrado Corazón, I teach undergraduate courses in face-to-face and online settings. My teaching work includes course content, Canvas implementation, learner activities, oral and written communication tasks, assessments, rubrics, feedback, discussion, presentations, argumentation, and analytical writing.

I also work with language laboratories and have taught multiple sections across GCM 102, GCM 112, and GCM 201. These courses involve communication, critical analysis, bilingual academic communication, rhetoric, argumentation, audience awareness, evidence-based reasoning, and applied oral and written practice.

Teaching allows me to observe how learners interpret directions, where they encounter ambiguity, how assessment criteria shape performance, how course organization affects access and progression, and how well instructional materials support the tasks students are actually expected to complete.

That perspective reinforces my approach to instructional design: objectives, explanations, practice, assessment, technology, and learner expectations need to operate as parts of the same system.

Language & Content

Editing and translation sharpened the way I evaluate instructional materials.

Since 2021, I have worked professionally with academic, curricular, instructional, and specialized content in English and Spanish. That work requires close attention to grammar, syntax, terminology, coherence, clarity, style, formatting, references, consistency, and the relationship between individual passages and the larger purpose of a document.

Those same concerns matter in instructional design. A course can contain accurate subject matter and still create difficulty if explanations are vague, terminology shifts between assets, directions do not match assessment requirements, examples are misaligned, or the sequence of information does not support the learner's next task.

My language background therefore contributes directly to course quality: I approach instructional content not only as information that must be correct, but as communication that must remain clear, consistent, usable, and appropriate for its audience.

Design Approach

Start with the need, then align the learning experience around it.

My approach begins by identifying what learners or performers need to understand or do, what currently prevents that performance, and what evidence would demonstrate successful learning or improvement.

From there, I consider the learner, task, context, objectives, instructional sequence, practice, assessment, technology, accessibility, and evaluation as connected design decisions. I draw on frameworks such as ADDIE, Bloom's taxonomy, ABCD objectives, Gagné's events of instruction, Merrill's First Principles, cognitive-load principles, ARCS, UDL, and other instructional-design models as appropriate to the problem.

I place particular value on instructional accuracy, alignment, learner-facing clarity, realistic practice, assessment validity, documentation, accessibility awareness, collaboration, quality assurance, and iteration.

The goal is not simply to produce course materials. It is to create a learning experience in which the content, activities, assessment, technology, and learner expectations support the same intended outcome.
