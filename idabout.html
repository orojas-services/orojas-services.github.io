About

My path into instructional design grew from working directly with courses, content, and learners.

I am a bilingual higher-education professional with more than five years of experience working with academic and instructional content. My background combines online course development and quality assurance, university teaching, English–Spanish editing and translation, Canvas course management, assessment, and collaboration across course-development teams.

Course Development

Working inside the instructional design process

My experience with instructional design developed through sustained work reviewing and refining academic, curricular, and instructional materials in higher education.

I have participated in course-development and review workflows alongside instructional designers, curriculum specialists, subject-matter experts, editors, and quality-assurance personnel. My work has included reviewing learner-facing content for language, terminology, coherence, accuracy, instructional consistency, institutional standards, and other quality concerns.

That work has given me practical experience with the iterative nature of course development: identifying issues, coordinating revisions, checking consistency across instructional assets, documenting changes, and supporting continuous improvement.

Teaching Perspective

I also see instructional decisions from the learner-facing side.

As an Adjunct Professor of English Communication at Universidad del Sagrado Corazón, I teach undergraduate courses in face-to-face and online settings. I work directly with course content, learner activities, Canvas, assessments, rubrics, feedback, and student performance.

Teaching gives me a perspective that complements course-development work. I see how instructions are interpreted, where learners encounter confusion, how assessment design affects performance, and how the organization of content can support or interfere with learning.

I use that experience to approach instructional design not simply as content production, but as the deliberate connection among what learners need to understand, what they are asked to do, how they practice, and how learning is evaluated.

Language & Content

Editing and translation strengthened the way I approach instructional content.

My professional background in English–Spanish editing and translation has trained me to examine instructional materials closely: terminology, structure, tone, consistency, audience, meaning, and the relationship between individual pieces of content and the larger message they are intended to communicate.

That attention to language is especially useful in instructional design, where a course can be conceptually sound but still fail learners if its explanations, directions, terminology, or sequence are unclear.

How I Work

Accuracy, alignment, clarity, and learner needs

My approach to instructional design is grounded in understanding the learning or performance need first, then considering the learner, instructional context, content, activities, assessment, and technology as parts of the same system.

I place particular value on instructional accuracy, clear communication, alignment, careful review, documentation, accessibility awareness, collaboration, and iteration.

The goal is not simply to produce course materials. It is to create learning experiences in which the content, structure, technology, and learner expectations work together coherently.
