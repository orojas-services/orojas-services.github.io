About

My path into instructional design grew from working directly with courses, content, and learners.

I am a bilingual higher-education professional with more than five years of experience working with academic and instructional content. My path into instructional design combines course-development and quality-assurance work, university teaching, Canvas-based instruction, assessment, and a professional background in English–Spanish editing and translation.

That combination gives me experience with both the development side of learning and the learner-facing side: how instructional materials are reviewed, corrected, coordinated, and maintained, and how learners encounter directions, activities, assessments, feedback, technology, and course structure in practice.

Course Development

Working inside higher-education course-development and quality-assurance workflows

My instructional design experience developed through sustained work reviewing and refining academic, curricular, instructional, and multimedia materials in higher education.

I work within course-development processes involving instructional designers, curriculum specialists, subject-matter experts, editors, quality-assurance personnel, course builders, and academic leadership. My responsibilities include reviewing learner-facing materials for conceptual accuracy, terminology, language, coherence, instructional clarity, formatting, consistency, institutional standards, and alignment across related course assets.

I also support learning-resource quality and compliance work by identifying potential copyright and intellectual-property concerns, coordinating resources for quality review, implementing recommended corrections, and maintaining documentation supporting learning-resource validation.

The work is iterative rather than limited to a single editing pass. It can involve identifying a defect, determining what other assets are affected, coordinating or incorporating revisions, checking consistency across materials, documenting the change, and verifying the corrected learner-facing result.

Cross-Functional Course Development

Academic Quality & Standards

Teaching Perspective

I also see instructional decisions from the learner-facing side.

As an Adjunct Professor of English Communication at Universidad del Sagrado Corazón, I teach undergraduate courses in both online and face-to-face modalities. My work includes oral and written communication, academic expression, rhetorical analysis, argumentation, evidence-based reasoning, presentations, discussions, analytical writing, assessments, rubrics, feedback, and Canvas-supported course delivery.

I design and facilitate presentations, written assignments, guided practice, reflective tasks, discussions, debates, simulations, role-playing, and other learner-centered activities that require students to apply communication concepts rather than only review them.

Teaching allows me to observe how learners interpret directions, where ambiguity becomes a barrier, how assessment criteria affect performance, how sequencing shapes practice, and how Canvas requirements and course organization influence the learner path.

That perspective reinforces my approach to instructional design: instructional goals, explanations, learner practice, assessment, feedback, technology, and course structure need to support the same intended outcome.

Language & Content

Editing and translation sharpened the way I evaluate instructional materials.

Since 2021, I have edited, reviewed, and translated academic, curricular, instructional, and specialized materials in English and Spanish. That work requires close attention to grammar, orthography, syntax, terminology, style, coherence, clarity, formatting, references, consistency, meaning, and document-level quality.

I also review bilingual and learner-facing content for conceptual integrity, terminology consistency, instructional clarity, and alignment with the intended academic context.

Those same concerns matter in instructional design. A course can contain accurate subject matter and still create difficulty if its explanations are vague, terminology changes across assets, directions do not match assessment requirements, examples are misaligned, or the sequence of information does not prepare the learner for the next task.

My language background therefore contributes directly to course quality: I approach instructional content not only as information that must be correct, but as communication that must remain clear, consistent, usable, and appropriate for its audience.

Design Approach

Start with the need, then align the learning experience around it.

My approach begins by identifying what learners or performers need to understand or do, what currently prevents that performance, and what evidence would demonstrate successful learning or improvement.

From there, I consider the learner, task, context, objectives, instructional sequence, practice, assessment, technology, accessibility, and evaluation as connected design decisions.

My instructional-design preparation includes frameworks and approaches such as ADDIE, learner and task analysis, prerequisite analysis, ABCD objectives, Bloom's taxonomy, Gagné's events of instruction, Merrill's First Principles, Dick and Carey, cognitive-load principles, ARCS, UDL, transactional distance, and other models that can be applied according to the needs of the learning problem.

I place particular value on instructional accuracy, alignment, learner-facing clarity, realistic practice, assessment validity, documentation, accessibility awareness, collaboration, quality assurance, and iteration.

The goal is not simply to produce course materials. It is to create a learning experience in which content, activities, assessment, technology, and learner expectations support the same intended outcome.
