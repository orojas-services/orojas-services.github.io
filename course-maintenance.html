Correcting an Instructional Content Model Across Canvas and Teaching Materials

A course-maintenance case study showing how I identified a substantive instructional-content defect, corrected the learner-facing Canvas content, revised the related instructional materials and practice activities, and verified consistency across the updated course assets.

The Problem

A content-structure inconsistency could teach learners the wrong model

During review of an undergraduate communication course, I identified a substantive issue in the way the course presented the structure of classical argumentation.

The existing materials represented the sequence as:

Introduction → Confirmation → Concession → Refutation → Conclusion

The material also combined concepts that should have been treated as distinct stages and presented concession as an independent structural component rather than as part of the refutation process.

Because the same conceptual model appeared across learner-facing materials, the issue was not simply a wording error. Left uncorrected, it could create conceptual confusion and carry an inaccurate structure into later practice and assessment.

Analysis

Determine the root problem before editing the course

Content Accuracy

The issue affected the conceptual model being taught, not merely grammar, terminology, or formatting.

Cross-Asset Consistency

Canvas content and instructional presentation materials needed to communicate the same corrected structure.

Learner Impact

An inaccurate sequence could interfere with learners' ability to recognize and construct arguments using the intended rhetorical framework.

Instructional Practice

Practice activities also needed to reinforce the corrected model so that the revision extended beyond explanatory content.

My Process

From defect identification to verified correction

1. Identify the Defect

Reviewed the instructional sequence and identified that the existing structure did not accurately represent the intended classical argument model.

2. Correct the Canvas Content

Updated the learner-facing course material so the corrected structure would become the authoritative version presented in the online course.

3. Revise Instructional Materials

Rebuilt the corresponding instructional presentation to separate the stages correctly and align its terminology with the revised Canvas content.

4. Update Learner Practice

Revised application activities so learners practiced identifying and constructing the corrected argument structure rather than merely reviewing terminology.

5. Verify Consistency

Reviewed the updated Canvas content and instructional materials together to confirm that the core treatment was aligned across assets.

6. Preserve the Change

Treated the correction as a course-maintenance issue requiring a traceable before-and-after state rather than as an isolated edit.

Corrected Instructional Model

The revised structure

The corrected course treatment presented the classical sequence as:

Exordium → Narratio → Confirmatio → Refutatio → Peroratio

The revised lesson separated Narratio from Exordium and treated concession as a rhetorical move that may occur within Refutatio rather than as a separate classical stage.

Outcome

A consistent instructional model across the course experience

The corrected structure was incorporated into both the Canvas content and the corresponding instructional materials. Practice activities were also updated to reinforce the revised model.

A later consistency review confirmed that the core Canvas and presentation treatment were aligned.

This case did not include a controlled measure of learning improvement, so I do not attribute changes in student performance directly to the revision. The documented outcome was correction of the instructional defect and restoration of consistency across course assets.
